# Apple IVR reliability investigation — 26 October 2026

## Evidence before new tests

The supplied Ginza report described a transmitted digit 5. Independent inspection contradicts that claim: the model used the exact token from its successful menu read, but the selection returned `STALE_OR_INCOMPLETE_MENU`; the complete carrier event list for the uniquely matched call leg has **no send_dtmf command**. The 57-second call ended remotely. A model tool invocation must never be reported as a transmitted tone when the tool failed.

The later Shibuya call is a different failure: the carrier recorded one 250 ms command approximately 49 seconds after answer, without an observed transfer before remote hangup. Its transcript suggests an invalid-input announcement; that wording alone does not prove a carrier transport failure. Kawasaki's latest 139-second call recorded one command 27.7 seconds after answer and reached a recipient who answered the inventory question. Backend completion is not commercial success, and API acceptance is not remote IVR acceptance. [Telnyx documents](https://developers.telnyx.com/api-reference/call-commands/send-dtmf) the command, 100–500 ms digit duration, command-id deduplication and absence of an associated delivery webhook. No transmission-method setting is changed.

## Reproduction and correction

A no-call, real-time replay of the Ginza inbound recording through Nova-3 Japanese reproduced the segmented introduction/options and a repeated partial introduction. Regression fixtures additionally reproduce a committed STT message containing both `五を押してください。` and the start of the next `次の四つの中から` announcement. Previously, the new option count discarded the just-received final choice. Another fixture shows that a partial introduction replaced a complete immutable snapshot between read and selection.

The controller now finishes the old menu before processing the bundled replay introduction. An exactly observed replay prefix can preserve the original unconsumed snapshot and token until its original expiry; it cannot renew the lifetime, retarget a queued digit or create a new token for an incomplete menu. Recognition uses the already-observed counted introduction rather than requiring the recognizer to spell the entire invitation perfectly. Changed counts, new/extended options, quoted speech, human questions, holds, sensitive choices, unknown introductions and expired snapshots still invalidate or block execution. Generic unverified replay introductions no longer preserve a queued selection.

The private conversation metadata retains the last four compact gate results, without raw tokens, transcripts or credentials, so a failed selection is not overwritten by later reads. It is diagnostic only, with no role in OAuth or consent. Full snapshots, audio and private identities remain ignored by git.

## Handoff evidence and bounded follow-up

The updated Kawasaki call received one accepted 250 ms command 34.4 seconds after answer, using fresh matching-replay evidence, then an observed transfer. It ended remotely after 152 seconds. Its live transcript did not expose a staff greeting before termination. Independent REST retranscription of the recorded inbound audio **does** contain a staff greeting and an explicit statement that the staff could not hear the caller before hanging up. Thus the original live-only interpretation of an unanswered queue was incomplete. No availability or booking was established.

The second Ginza call dispatched one command 38.2 seconds after answer and entered routing. It ended after 227 seconds at the bounded routing timeout. A matching signed `call.speak.ended` completed event precedes the hangup command by approximately 1.26 seconds; carrier termination is caller/normal clearing. No commercial response or reservation was confirmed. These are four new calls, alternating Ginza, Marunouchi, Kawasaki, Ginza.

A deterministic fixture shows that a direct staff greeting such as `お電話ありがとうございます。アップル川崎です。` was not recognized as a return from hold without `もしもし`; a bundled earlier transfer announcement could retain the hold. The return detector now accepts the direct telephone greeting and the relevant store introductions as **dialogue candidates**, honors cue order and any subsequent request to keep waiting, and rejects quotations. It neither asserts verified human identity nor authorizes new actions. Bridge fixtures require the hold to release and a short permitted response to reach the media transport.

The same post-routing recording was replayed in real time without dialing. Nova-3 streaming recognized the final audibility/closure statement but missed the earlier greeting. On its last 45 seconds, fixed gain 4 introduced 3,795 clipped samples and a poorly recognized greeting (confidence 0.444); peak-limited normalization produced confidence 0.220. Flux-multi yielded mixed Portuguese/Japanese instead of a reliable greeting. **None of those model or gain alternatives was deployed.** This small comparison exposes a remaining ASR limitation and does not certify recognition accuracy, low latency or the cause of every missed greeting. No speculative endpointing parameter or unconditional release of hold is introduced.

Diagnostics now distinguish a token checked at dispatch from a different menu that may arrive while the provider HTTP request is pending. A new snapshot during that request cannot retroactively invalidate an already reserved and accepted command. An automated race fixture covers the distinction; it cannot weaken action validation or authorize a replay.

The final handoff/diagnostic change passed 232/232 tests, type checking, 24+29 phone-contract checks, all 11 workerd checks, all builds and the production dry run. Only the voice controller is deployed. Credit was verified available after the owner's recharge notification; exact balances and credentials are not recorded in this report. The five-per-hour execution quota remains unchanged. Further real calls are deferred until 15:00 JST and remain within the six-call ceiling. A preparatory tool call before that time was rejected by automatic review and was not worked around or executed.

## First-response cancellation loop and offline correction

After 15:00 JST, the fifth new call reached Marunouchi staff following one 250 ms selection, 31.5 seconds after answer. Direct greetings now resumed the dialogue. The staff repeatedly checked audibility while each model response was cancelled before completion. The call ended remotely after 96 seconds, with no inventory answer or reservation. An independent channel measurement of the final 36 seconds found inbound peak 17,831/RMS 1,429.84 and outbound peak **0**/RMS **0**. Generated assistant transcript fragments were not transmitted speech.

The full-utterance safety buffer correctly withheld unfinished/unvalidated audio. However, treating every repeated `もしもし` as a new interruption could cancel that buffer indefinitely. The new policy makes the first post-transfer response a single short acknowledgement. Only during that first server-bound response, for at most ten seconds from its original creation time, exclusive audibility checks may let that response finish instead of restarting generation. The deadline is never renewed. Real questions, task details, corrections, requests to wait or stop, quotations, later responses, IVR, holds, terminal state and policy rejection retain their cancellation/blocking rules. No early/unvalidated audio is released and no consent or action permission is inferred from these checks.

The timing fixture uses approximate offsets from the recorded assistant entry (1.59 and 6.77 seconds), not an inferred model-latency measurement. Two checks generate zero cancellations of the first response. Buffered audio remains absent until successful final-transcript validation; a nonzero 0.2-second synthetic signal then emits exactly ten 20 ms media frames. Other tests require meaningful interruptions and later-response checks to cancel normally and the ten-second limit to expire. This establishes transport/controller behavior with mocks, not recipient-side audibility or successful PSTN dialogue.

235/235 automated tests passed, with type checking, the unchanged 24+29 phone-contract and 11 workerd checks, builds and production dry run. The owner then explicitly paused calls for an hour and authorized a subsequent separate batch of five distinct stores. No new real calls are made while completing this offline correction. Recognition limitations from the earlier recorded-audio comparison remain; no model, gain, Telnyx configuration, inbound service or authorization guard is changed.

## Authorized five-store resumption

The owner explicitly approved the separate five-store batch after the pause. By approval receipt, the proposed one-time start had already elapsed; no earlier automatic run had occurred and all prior calls were terminal. The continuation therefore uses the current chat and an exclusive persistent five-attempt ledger, not a recurring schedule. Each store can be called at most once in that batch, with an early stop after any confirmed free reservation.

The first resumed Ginza call ended remotely after 57 seconds, without a carrier DTMF command. Its observed transcription included numeric `ニを` instead of `二を`. The strict four-choice coverage gate therefore could not recognize all choices. A shared menu-text normalizer now accepts the recorded kana forms `ニ` and `ゴ`/`ゴー` only following a menu-like boundary and immediately before `を` or `番を`. It does not rewrite words such as `カニ` or `ゴール`, accept unknown hiragana or invent an unheard option. The exact token, full counted coverage, sensitive-menu block, one-use reservation, expiry and replay controls remain required. The same normalization prevents a kana-only recognition variation from being misreported as a new menu. Ginza is not retried in this batch.

The kana refinement passed 238/238 tests, type checking, 24+29 phone-contract checks, all 11 workerd checks, all builds and the production configuration dry run. No actual call was made by these tests. Deployment remains limited to the outbound voice controller.
